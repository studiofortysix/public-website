Specialist service business

## A completion event was not a confirmed customer. {#audit}

Completed: independent review, findings report and supporting evidence.
Further repairs were recommendations.

### The question

Could website reporting reliably explain enquiries, customer registrations and payments?

### My role

I reviewed the analytics and tracking configuration, compared website journeys with the business definitions, and tested selected behaviour in a controlled copy of the website.

### What I found and delivered

Some events described website activity rather than a dependable count of confirmed customers. The written audit distinguished accepted enquiries, customer confirmation and different payment categories. It set out the evidence, limitations and repairs to prioritise.

### Why the distinction mattered

A browser event alone could not establish every business outcome. The recommendations identified where operational records were needed and where tracking could be improved. The audit did not establish a revenue increase or a completed implementation.

Professional regulators

## Separate websites. One reporting structure. {#regulators}

Completed: a dashboard with two reporting views and a definitions page.
No quantified business-impact claim is made.

### The question

How could the team understand website performance using a report that was useful for recurring reporting and governance questions?

### My role

I developed the reporting dashboard and worked through the team’s questions about the metrics and their interpretation.

### What I delivered

The completed report brought the two website views into one location, with a separate definitions page. Feedback on the draft informed the explanation of the measures. A limitation in the available search-term history was explicitly recorded.

### What this demonstrates

The value was in the combination of a usable reporting view, clear definitions and documented source coverage. It shows completed reporting delivery for professional regulators without implying a wider government endorsement or claiming an unmeasured performance improvement.

Agency collaboration

## Portal tracking checked before release. {#agency}

Completed: GTM configuration and GA4 validation in staging.
The later production release is outside this example.

### The question

An agency was updating a professional regulator’s practitioner portal and needed selected page and course activity recorded in GA4.

### My role

I worked with the developer on the event requirements, configured the relevant GTM variables, triggers and tags, and checked their behaviour on the staging website.

### What I delivered

The selected events were confirmed as firing to GA4 in the test environment. The handover clearly identified that point of validation and the need for a further check when the agency released the update.

### What this demonstrates

Specialist tracking support can fit into an existing agency and development workflow. Clear communication about what has been tested is part of the deliverable.
